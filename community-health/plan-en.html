Community health awareness initiative: concept and implementation plan

Faculty of Nursing, Tobruk University | Implementation proposal led by the Community Service and Environment Department, with student participation and faculty supervision | Version 0.1 | 15 October 2026

The idea and intended outcome

Create a free, simple web app that serves community members on their phones, translating what nursing students learn into understandable awareness and supervised community practice. Users can choose a topic, understand suitable daily steps, recognise when to seek help, and share or print the material.

Initial hosting on GitHub Pages is proposed, followed by moving the same files into a folder within the faculty portal on the university website. The university address becomes the official reference when ready. The proposed name can be changed without changing the structure.

Educational and community value

  • For the community: understandable, actionable knowledge that respects culture and available resources and supports seeking care early.
  • For students: practice in health communication, finding sources, simplifying information, teamwork, listening and evaluation.
  • For the faculty and university: ongoing, institutionally owned community service that documents students' actual contributions and connects activities to people's needs.

Available app features

A phone-friendly interface, 34 fields and 197 health guides, search, favourites, printing and sharing, installation and offline-reading files, student guidance, downloadable material drafts for review, and content and activity registers. The app also includes 32 branching scenarios, 12 stories and 100 library resources. No activities, beneficiary counts or reviewer names are invented.

The current health materials are initial texts based on official sources. Their scientific and language review within the faculty and documented publication authorisation form part of the process before official initiative campaigns. Review status is separate from the app's technical readiness.

Selecting a new priority

The department selects a topic based on how often the need occurs, the severity of preventable harm, the possibility of changing a simple behaviour, availability of a source and reviewer, and implementation cost. Needs emerging from visits and questions take priority over increasing page counts. The department sets later numerical targets after evaluating the pilot.

The user experience and material structure

A short journey from a phone to a manageable action

Users open the app without registration, then choose a field or enter a word such as blood pressure or pregnancy. A guide presents a core message, practical steps, actions to avoid, signs for seeking care, the nursing role, sources and review status. Users can save it on their device, share its link or print it.

Material template for review

  • A clear title, defined audience and one observable behavioural goal.
  • A main message in simple Arabic, followed by three short steps wherever possible.
  • A common action to avoid, signs for seeking help and the degree of urgency.
  • The student's and nurse's role in explanation, support and referral, and the activity's boundaries.
  • The official source, link, access date and material version.
  • Participating students and their consent to show names, the reviewer and their role and review date, the publication authoriser and the update date.

Formats for community activities

Material readable within minutes, a printable sheet and a brief practical demonstration when appropriate. Human audio recordings and original or licensed illustrations can be added later. QR codes on publications link to the material's official address once the institutional link is approved.

Accessibility and language

Arabic was the initial language of the community content, with a clear font, good contrast, large buttons, adjustable text size and alternative text for illustrations. Content avoids unexplained terminology, fear and stigma. Draft-preparation guidance for students and supervisors is in Arabic and English to involve faculty members who do not speak Arabic. The full English translation requires an equivalent level of review.

Limits of the free version

The current version does not require an AI service, accounts or a database. External links still need a connection. Installation and offline reading depend on the browser and a successful first download; they should be tested on users' actual devices before an official announcement.

Review pathway

Defined need, assignment, sourced draft, faculty review, revision, small comprehension test, publication authorisation, release and follow-up. Writing or downloading a draft does not mean it has been submitted or authorised. The department keeps approvals in its usual institutional location, and only suitable public material is published.

Quality control and prompt correction

A proposed review interval is every six months, or immediately when a source or recommendation changes. First-aid and outbreak content receives faster attention when guidance changes. If a consequential error is found, withdraw or correct the material immediately, document the change and publish a new version. Test updates to content stored on phones after each release.

The department names a lead and deputy for each function; one person may perform several functions according to resources. Publication authorisation must involve actual reading and recorded comments rather than a nominal name. Do not publish a student's name or activity photographs containing people without appropriate consent.

A practical eight-week pilot plan

The weeks start when the team is assigned; they are not predetermined dates. A starting group of 4 to 6 students, one faculty reviewer and a Community Service Department coordinator is proposed, using available devices and communication.

Proposed first campaign

Reducing salt and informed blood pressure follow-up. Use the salt, blood pressure and food guides, read labels on available products and discuss one step a family can take. Measuring blood pressure or buying a monitor is not required for a successful campaign. If an activity includes actual measurements, the supervisor approves competence, equipment, method and the referral pathway beforehand.

Effort and resource estimate

Flexible proposal: 60 to 90 minutes per student weekly, and 30 to 45 minutes of weekly supervisory review according to content. Organised time, an initial connection and an available phone are needed. Printing, photography, audio and transport are optional according to resources, not conditions for running the app. The plan assumes no funding or new appointments.

Measuring benefit, learning and improvement

Measurement distinguishes what was produced, what reached people and what changed in their understanding or behaviour. Page or visit counts do not prove a health impact. The department starts with a small measurement it can implement and follow through.

Salt and blood pressure campaign example

Before and after the activity, the supervisor asks three identical questions: Can blood pressure be high without symptoms? What is a practical alternative to adding a lot of salt? Can medicine be stopped when you feel better? The faculty member approves the wording and answer key before use. Each correct answer earns one point, for a total from zero to three. A random paper code can match the two responses without names.

The optional follow-up asks whether one specific step was taken, such as tasting food before adding salt, and about one barrier. No medical data is collected in the app. If contact details are needed for follow-up, the department keeps them separately under controlled access and sets a deletion date. Do not upload them to the repository or publication records.

One improvement decision

The team records what was not understood or applied and why, then changes an example, illustration or explanation and repeats a short question in a later activity. Report counts and denominators without generalising a limited pilot to the whole Tobruk community. No actual results are recorded in this version.

Hosting, institutional ownership and continuity

Proposed technical approach

The app consists of static HTML, CSS and JavaScript files, with an app manifest and a service worker for offline caching. It can be hosted on GitHub Pages in the available public repository and then copied to a path within the university website. Current functions need no application server, database or paid keys.

What to request from the university IT department

  • A folder under the university domain, HTTPS and a permanent proposed path such as nursing/community-health, without assuming the final address.
  • Allow JavaScript, JSON, webmanifest files and a service worker only within the app folder, with correct MIME types.
  • A limited-permission update method for the lead and deputy, or a handover process through which IT publishes releases.
  • Avoid leaving the service worker cached for a long time without updates, and test version changes and offline reading on Android and iPhone.
  • A backup and recovery method, with university documentation of account ownership and permissions.

Operating cost

The files can run on GitHub Pages in a public repository under its free plan and usage limits. Existing university hosting is an alternative if IT agrees. Free here means no software subscription is needed for the app; it does not remove users' connection costs, team time, printing or optional production costs.

Handover and sustainability

The faculty keeps the code, content, sources, version log, approvals, update guide, backup and permission-transfer information in institutional custody. Passwords do not belong in public files. Transfer administration to an institutional account or team when available, with a deputy who tests updating a guide and restoring a copy.

Students' names appear on their actual contributions with consent, while ownership and continuity of the initiative remain with the faculty and university. Respect source and logo rights: write original awareness material with attribution and do not copy protected books or images. Any later central electronic receipt of drafts or evaluations needs a permissions and storage design separate from public pages.

Activation, follow-up and attached templates

The faculty's next steps

  • Name a Community Service Department coordinator, faculty reviewer and technical deputy, and confirm the proposed name.
  • Select the first campaign's audience and a student group suited to the available time.
  • Review initial materials and record approvals progressively while continuing the remaining development.
  • Verify the emergency contact and referral pathway locally before presenting them to the public.
  • Test the app on real phones, then authorise the initiative's official publication and reference link.

Attached registers

The content review register has one row for each material, including title, field, source, status and fields for students, reviewers, dates and publication approval. Reviewer and approval fields are blank. The activity and impact register is an empty bilingual template for aggregate counts, measurement, follow-up and improvement; it contains no patient data.

Acceptance checklist before official launch

Complete review of published materials, visible status for each, correct links, functioning search, printing and sharing, phone-friendly display, successful first download and offline updates, no invented names or indicators, and a named lead, deputy and correction pathway. Browser display and actual installation need checking on faculty devices.
